How a race weekend works

Three days, one result. Here is what to watch and when it matters.

Friday

Practice. Teams test setups and tyres while the drivers learn the track.

  • Lap times are not the full story
  • Watch the long runs for race pace

Saturday

Qualifying decides the starting grid. One perfect lap is everything.

  • Three knockout sessions
  • Pole position is a big advantage

Sunday

The race. Strategy, tyre wear, and safety cars decide who wins.

  • Points go to the top ten
  • Every pit stop can change the order

Sprint weekends swap some of this around, so check the official schedule for each race.

Terms you will hear on the radio

Tap a term for a plain-English explanation.

Undercut

Pitting before the car ahead so fresh tyres give you faster laps. If it works, you come out in front when they stop.

Box, box

The team telling a driver to come into the pits on the next lap.

Parc fermé

Rules that limit what teams can change on the car after qualifying starts, so the setup is largely locked in for the race.

Safety car and virtual safety car

The safety car leads the field at a slow pace after an incident. The virtual safety car makes everyone slow down without bunching up the field.

Delta

The time gap to another car or to a target lap time. A driver asked to "hit the delta" must stay on a set pace.
